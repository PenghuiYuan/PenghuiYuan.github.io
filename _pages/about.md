I am an M.S. student in Computer Technology at Beijing Information Science and Technology University, advised by Prof. Qiang Tong (佟强). Since September 2024, I have also been a joint master's research student at the Visual Media Research Center, Tsinghua University. I conduct research with Prof. [Song-Hai Zhang](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=AWtV-EQAAAAJ) (张松海) from Tsinghua University and Prof. [Sen-Zhe Xu](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Sen-Zhe-Xu) (胥森哲) from the University of Science and Technology Beijing.

My research focuses on Redirected Walking (RDW) and Virtual Reality (VR), particularly trajectory forecasting, cybersickness-aware gain control, and audiovisual spatial perception. My work includes developing Unity-based VR experimental systems, conducting user studies, evaluating prediction models, and analyzing experimental data.

# 🔬 Research Experience
- **Joint Master's Research Student, Visual Media Research Center, Tsinghua University** — 2024.09 - Present
  - Conduct research on trajectory prediction, reference-frame selection, cybersickness-aware RDW gain control, and audiovisual spatial perception in immersive VR.
  - Develop Unity-based VR experimental systems and data collection, preprocessing, trajectory analysis, and statistical evaluation pipelines.
  - Collaborate on proactive reset strategies, RDW gain optimization, and audiovisual perception.

- **Predictor-Dependent Reference-Frame Selection for Trajectory Forecasting under Redirected Walking** — IEEE ISMAR
  - Collected paired virtual-environment (VE) and physical-environment (PE) trajectories from 25 participants across four RDW conditions.
  - Evaluated SingularTrajectory, AgentFormer, PECNet, and EKF with subject-wise five-fold cross-validation, zero-shot transfer, and fine-tuning.
  - Found that learning-based predictors generally favored PE coordinates, whereas EKF favored VE coordinates. Mixed-frame fine-tuning reduced AgentFormer's normalized average displacement error by 22.2% in both VE and PE compared with the zero-shot baseline.

- **Online Cybersickness Estimation and Closed-Loop Gain Control** — IEEE VR, Under Review
  - Developed an LSTM-based online cybersickness estimator using physical and virtual motion data, updating Fast Motion Sickness Scale (FMS) estimates every 0.5 seconds.
  - Designed a closed-loop controller that dynamically adjusts RDW translation gains according to estimated FMS levels.
  - In a study with 20 participants, reduced mean FMS by 51.5% and the change in Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ) Total score by 57.7% compared with conventional ZigZag redirection.
